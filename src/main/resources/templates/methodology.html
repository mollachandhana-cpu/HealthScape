TRANSPARENCY BY DESIGN

Know what the data can—and cannot—say.

HealthScapeAI brings environmental estimates and structured local reports into one interface. This page explains the data types, interpretation logic and limitations so users can distinguish a model signal from a verified observation.

PRODUCT PRINCIPLEExplainable · Not diagnostic

Data sources

Each source has a different coverage and confidence profile.

A
Open-Meteo weather forecast

Current model conditions and hourly forecasts for temperature, apparent temperature, humidity, precipitation, wind, gusts, UV, visibility and cloud cover. Coverage and available fields vary by location/model.

Weather API documentation ↗

B
Open-Meteo air-quality forecast

Modelled AQI and pollutant estimates including PM2.5, PM10, ozone, nitrogen dioxide, sulphur dioxide, carbon monoxide and dust where returned. Values are not guaranteed to match a nearby regulatory station.

Air quality API documentation ↗

C
Community and field observations

Authenticated staff can record reports such as suspected water contamination, poor drainage or sanitation, mosquito activity and heat-related concerns. Reports are user-entered and require operational verification.

Open field observations ↗

D
AI briefing (optional)

When configured with a server-side Gemini API key, the application can generate a narrative from supplied environmental metrics. Without the key, explainable rule-based insights remain available. AI output is not medical advice.

How risk signals are generated

Threshold-based flags are transparent heuristics, not a clinically validated prediction model.

Environmental dimensions considered

Air pollution: US AQI and PM2.5 thresholds help surface potential exposure concerns. Additional pollutant concentrations provide context but are not combined into an invented composite score.

Heat and UV: actual/apparent temperature, humidity and UV index are checked against explicit rule thresholds to create practical awareness messages.

Weather disruption: rain probability/precipitation, wind gusts and visibility can trigger planning notes. These signals do not constitute a flood forecast or official severe-weather warning.

Community health: water-quality, sanitation and field-report information comes from the existing operational workflow and should be treated as local records—not a live sensor feed unless separately verified.

Responsible-use boundaries

Important before making a public-health, clinical or emergency decision.

What this product does not claim

It does not diagnose illness, prove that a pollutant caused a symptom, replace a regulatory monitoring station, certify drinking water, autonomously predict outbreaks, or replace emergency alerts. Modelled values may be delayed, incomplete, spatially smoothed or unavailable.

For urgent health or safety decisions, consult qualified professionals and official local public-health, environmental and meteorological authorities. Do not use outdoor modelled carbon monoxide as an indoor carbon monoxide detector.

Data quality note: the app deliberately displays unavailable fields as unavailable instead of silently substituting zero. Historical database entries represent saved refresh snapshots, not a complete authoritative history of environmental measurements.
